DESIGN EQUATIONS - PRECISION PEAK DETECTOR CIRCUIT
===================================================

=================================================================================
EQUATION 1: TIME CONSTANT FOR PEAK-HOLD CIRCUIT
=================================================================================

Formula: τ = R × C

Where:
  τ (tau) = Time constant in seconds
  R = Resistance in ohms
  C = Capacitance in farads

Meaning:
  After time τ, the capacitor charges to 63.2% of input voltage
  After time τ, the capacitor discharges to 36.8% of stored voltage
  After 5τ, the capacitor is essentially fully charged/discharged

Application to this project:
  The peak-hold stage must hold the detected peak long enough to measure it
  but discharge slowly enough to reset for the next peak detection


=================================================================================
EQUATION 2: SIGNAL FREQUENCY SPECIFICATION
=================================================================================

Formula: f = 1 Hz

Where:
  f = Frequency in Hz (cycles per second)

Specification:
  Biomedical signals (ECG/EEG) typically operate at low frequencies
  Selected frequency: 1 Hz (slow biomedical signal)
  This represents heart rate or brain wave monitoring applications

Real-world context:
  Typical ECG/EEG frequencies: 0.05 - 1 Hz
  Selected test frequency: 1 Hz (conservative, safe upper limit)


=================================================================================
EQUATION 3: TIME PERIOD CALCULATION
=================================================================================

Formula: T = 1 / f

Calculation:
  T = 1 / 1 Hz
  T = 1 second

Where:
  T = Time period (duration of one complete cycle)
  f = Frequency

Meaning:
  One complete oscillation of the sine wave takes 1 second
  Signal goes UP for 0.5 seconds, then DOWN for 0.5 seconds

Application:
  The peak-hold circuit must hold the value for at least one period
  so that measurement can occur before the next peak arrives


=================================================================================
EQUATION 4: TIME CONSTANT DESIGN CONSTRAINT
=================================================================================

Design Rule: τ ≥ 10 × T

Derivation:
  T = 1 second (signal period)
  τ ≥ 10 × 1 second
  τ ≥ 10 seconds

Purpose:
  Ensures that the capacitor discharges very slowly relative to signal period
  Allows the circuit to hold the peak value through multiple signal cycles
  Provides stability for measurement

Calculation of voltage remaining after one cycle:
  V_remaining = V_initial × e^(-T/τ)
  V_remaining = V_initial × e^(-1/10)
  V_remaining = V_initial × 0.905
  V_remaining = 90.5% of peak voltage

Result:
  Only 9.5% of charge is lost per signal cycle
  Peak value remains stable for accurate measurement


=================================================================================
EQUATION 5: CAPACITOR VALUE SELECTION
=================================================================================

Selection: C = 10 µF

Justification:
  - Practical, standard capacitor value (easily available)
  - Large enough to store peak voltage effectively
  - Small enough to charge/discharge at reasonable speeds
  - Good balance for biomedical signal processing
  - Typical value for analog peak detector circuits

Charging characteristics:
  Charges through diode D1: Time ~milliseconds (FAST)
  Discharges through R3: Time ~seconds (SLOW)
  This creates the peak-hold effect


=================================================================================
EQUATION 6: RESISTOR CALCULATION FOR PEAK-HOLD
=================================================================================

Formula: R = τ / C

Given Values:
  τ = 10 seconds (from Equation 4)
  C = 10 µF = 10 × 10⁻⁶ Farads

Calculation:
  R = τ / C
  R = 10 seconds / (10 × 10⁻⁶ Farads)
  R = 10 / (10 × 10⁻⁶)
  R = 10 / (0.00001)
  R = 1,000,000 Ω
  R = 1 × 10⁶ Ω
  R = 1 MΩ

Result:
  Required resistance: R3 = 1 MΩ (1 megaohm)

Verification:
  τ = R × C = 1 × 10⁶ × 10 × 10⁻⁶ = 10 seconds ✓


=================================================================================
EQUATION 7: RESISTOR R1 AND R2 DERIVATION (INPUT BIASING NETWORK)
=================================================================================

Design Constraint 1: Input Bias Current
----------------------------------------
Formula: V_offset = I_bias × R

LM358 Input Bias Current:
  I_bias ≈ 80 nanoamps (80 × 10⁻⁹ A)

Acceptable voltage offset:
  V_offset < 10 mV

Derivation:
  R < V_offset / I_bias
  R < 0.01V / (80 × 10⁻⁹ A)
  R < 125,000 Ω
  R < 125 kΩ

Therefore: Choose R ≤ 125 kΩ

Design Constraint 2: Input Divider Current
-------------------------------------------
Formula: I_divider = VCC / (R1 + R2)

Target current:
  I_divider ≈ 25 µA (reasonable compromise for biomedical applications)

Derivation:
  VCC / (R1 + R2) = 25 × 10⁻⁶ A
  5V / (R1 + R2) = 25 × 10⁻⁶ A
  (R1 + R2) = 5V / (25 × 10⁻⁶ A)
  (R1 + R2) = 200,000 Ω
  (R1 + R2) = 200 kΩ

For symmetrical design (R1 = R2):
  R1 = R2 = 100 kΩ

Verification:
  I_divider = 5V / 200kΩ = 25 µA ✓

Design Constraint 3: Noise Performance
----------------------------------------
Thermal noise formula:
  V_noise = √(4 k T R B)

Where:
  k = Boltzmann constant = 1.38 × 10⁻²³ J/K
  T = Temperature = 300K (room temperature)
  R = Resistance
  B = Bandwidth = 10 Hz (biomedical signal bandwidth)

For R = 100 kΩ:
  V_noise ≈ 36 µV (acceptable for biomedical signals)

For R = 1 MΩ:
  V_noise ≈ 114 µV (too high, excessive noise)

Therefore: Keep R ≤ 100 kΩ to minimize noise

Final Selection: R1 = R2 = 100 kΩ
-----------------------------------
Satisfies all three constraints:
  ✓ Constraint 1 (Bias Current): 100k < 125k
  ✓ Constraint 2 (Input Current): R1 + R2 = 200k, I_divider = 25µA
  ✓ Constraint 3 (Noise): 100k gives acceptable noise level


=================================================================================
EQUATION 8: DC BIAS VOLTAGE CALCULATION
=================================================================================

Formula: V_bias = VCC × R2 / (R1 + R2)

For single-supply operation on +5V:
  The circuit needs a DC reference point at the middle of the supply

Calculation:
  V_bias = 5V × 100k / (100k + 100k)
  V_bias = 5V × 100k / 200k
  V_bias = 5V × 0.5
  V_bias = 2.5V

Result:
  DC bias point is set at 2.5V (middle of +5V supply)

Purpose:
  Allows the op-amp to process both positive and negative AC signals
  around this 2.5V center point
  Enables weak biomedical signals to be measured without distortion


=================================================================================
EQUATION 9: INPUT SIGNAL SPECIFICATION
=================================================================================

Signal format: SINE(offset amplitude frequency)
  SINE(0.5 0.05 1)

Breaking down each parameter:

Offset = 0.5V
  The signal oscillates around 0.5V as the center point
  This is the DC bias level of the signal

Amplitude = 0.05V
  The signal swings ±0.05V from the center point
  0.05V = 50 mV (millivolts)

Frequency = 1 Hz
  One complete cycle per second
  Represents slow biomedical signal

Resulting signal:
  Minimum = 0.5V - 0.05V = 0.45V
  Maximum = 0.5V + 0.05V = 0.55V
  Period = 1 second

This represents realistic low-voltage biomedical signals


=================================================================================
EQUATION 10: SIMULATION TIME SPECIFICATION
=================================================================================

Formula: .tran 0 10

Where:
  .tran = Transient analysis command in LTspice
  0 = Start time (0 seconds)
  10 = Stop time (10 seconds)

Meaning:
  Run simulation for 10 seconds of real time
  Observe 10 complete cycles of the 1 Hz sine wave
  Sufficient time to see peak detection and holding behavior

Time period analysis:
  Number of cycles = Total time / Period
  Number of cycles = 10 seconds / 1 second
  Number of cycles = 10 complete waveform cycles ✓
